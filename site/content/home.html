{{tagline}}

{{product}} runs a traceroute from your own computer, or reads one you paste, and draws the path on a world map with a hop table beside it. Free and open source, for macOS, Windows and Linux.

Download Source on GitHub

Three rules

  1. Hostname first

    A router's DNS name usually says where it is: ae1.fra10 is Frankfurt. {{product}} reads it with CAIDA's Hoiho rules and carriers' own site codes before it ever asks an IP database.

  2. Physics checked

    A hop cannot be farther away than light in fibre travels in its round-trip time. A placement that breaks that bound is rejected, whatever the database says, and the table says why.

  3. Honest labels

    Every placement says where it came from. A hop known only to a country is drawn hollow, and a hop that cannot be placed is listed with the reason, never guessed.

Runs on your machine

The trace runs with your system's own tracert, traceroute or mtr. No account, no telemetry, no automatic update check. What is looked up, where and when, is listed on the privacy page.

Map, table, export

Hops appear as the trace runs. Click a marker to find its rows, or a row to find its marker. Export a PNG map, a PDF report or JSON, from the window or the command line.

Also in FalconEye

The same engine, routemap-engine, powers the Route Map tab of FalconEye, OSINTPH's web application.
